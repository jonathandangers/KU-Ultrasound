Monday Image Review

Orientation usually starts on your first Monday at Image Review (normally 8:00–10:00 AM, occasionally 8:30 or 9:00 AM). Bring an O2-capable laptop if you have one so you can open patient charts while reviewing scans.

Room changes weekly. Open the Image Review event on the calendar before heading in to see that week's room and directions.

  • 1012 Orr-Major Respiratory Classroom Outside main hospital

Daily Shift Duties

  • Check in and check out with the ultrasound attending(s) on shift with you.
  • Sign the dry-erase boards outside ED-37 (main doc box) and ED-08 (North Zone) with your name, today's date/shift hours, and Voalte or cell number. Erase your info before leaving so you aren't called off-shift.
  • Return and prep machines: Check at the start, middle, and end of your shift that every machine is back at its marked home (e.g., Return Me To Outside ED-08), tidy, stocked, and plugged in.
  • Borrowing a machine: If you move a machine into an empty trauma/stat bay or the NZ conference room for practice, leave a note on the machine's picture at its home spot and clear it when you return.
  • Dress & gear: Wear scrubs; skip the white coat (it catches gel and slows volunteer peer-scanning). Keep bags minimal or use medical school lockers.
  • Breaks: Plan on quick ~30-minute cafeteria runs during 8-hour shifts. Food and drink are fine at Image Review and Wednesday conference.
Infection & COVID precautions: Wear gloves, a mask, and eye protection in every patient room. Do not enter rooms for known or suspected COVID-19 or any droplet-precaution room (yellow gown). When unsure, ask your ultrasound attending first.

Training vs. Point-of-Care Studies

Both study types count toward your 200-scan minimum. Choose the right workflow on the ultrasound machine before you scan:

Training Study Patient gives permission to practice, or you scan a co-rotator. Not used for clinical decisions. Enter TRAIN in the attending box on the machine ("Training, Exam" in Qpath). Manual MRN entry. No charge to patient; does not enter the medical record or PACS. Finish Qpath sheet by end of day.

    Standard Cleaning (Before & After Every Study)

    1. Wipe off all ultrasound gel first with a hand towel or washcloth.
    2. Use a grey-top wipe (universally approved across ED machines; Venue Go also accepts purple or orange).
    3. Wipe from where the cord meets the machine outward to the probe, spending 30–60 seconds on the probe itself.

    Gross Contamination & Probe Covers

    • Probe covers: Required for all procedures (including vascular access) and any scan on or near non-intact skin, wounds, cellulitis, abscesses, or blood. Use sterile technique for sterile procedures like central lines.
    • High-level disinfection: If blood, pus, or bodily fluid gets directly on the probe, grab a patient sticker, disconnect the probe, remove gel/fluid with a towel, wipe with a grey-top wipe, and hand the probe + sticker to the Charge Nurse by the doc box.

    Qpath & Missing Studies

    Log in to Qpath after scanning to delete redundant clips, complete the clinical report, and submit for grading. Submit incomplete or imperfect studies too—you won't get credit, but you'll get feedback.

    Deadlines: Complete Point-of-Care (clinical) reports immediately after scanning. Complete Training study sheets by the end of the day.

    How to Log In

    • Via O2 (hospital or home): Log in to O2 (default department HC1 EMERGENCY) → Epic tab → Weblinks → Qpath.
    • Direct browser (hospital only): Open Chrome or Edge and navigate to qpath/.
    qpath/

    If Your Study Is Missing

    1. Wrong date: Check adjacent dates in Qpath under your name.
    2. Stuck on the machine: Weak WiFi or a dead battery can pause transfer. Go back to the machine and send manually (GE transfer video · PX transfer video).
    3. Username typo or blank: Ask an attending to locate the study in Qpath and attach your name under Operator. Contact Dr. Jackson if it's still missing.

    Vascular Access Rules

    • Wait for gel-model training first: Do not accept IV requests until you complete the rotation's gel-model session (you may observe or assist beforehand).
    • Direct supervision & limb alerts: Every student procedure must be directly observed in full by a resident or attending. Always verify safe extremities with the patient, RN, and treating team before placing a line.
    • Qpath exam type: Log practice lines as TRAIN - US GUIDED NEEDLE PLACEMENT. For difficult sticks requested by the team, have the team place a POC ED GUIDE VASCULAR ACCESS order and pull it from the machine's Work List.
    • Report wording: Select the dynamic guidance option (active needle-tip visualization—used for ~99% of ED lines) and document catheter gauge and length under Other Interpretation / Complication.
    • Failed or blown IVs: Still save images and log the study. Apply direct pressure and immediately tell the nurse and treating physician the exact site so no one places a subsequent IV distally.

    Syllabus & Final Deliverables

    Last Monday Image Review Deliverables

    Your final Image Review day typically includes three graded items: your case or article presentation, your 10 quiz questions, and the computerized end-of-rotation knowledge exam.

    1. Case or Article Presentation (5–10 min, PowerPoint)

    • ED Case option: Present a patient case from your rotation with exported Qpath images/clips and supporting literature (Qpath export video).
    • Journal Article option: Present an ultrasound paper with relevant images, and bring printed copies of the article for the group (usually 2–5 people).

    2. Ten Ultrasound Questions (PowerPoint)

    • Write 10 multiple-choice questions covering rotation material.
    • Include ultrasound images on at least half of the questions.

    Rotation Goals & Responsibilities

    Core Objectives

    • Explain ultrasound physics, knobology, indications, and exam limitations.
    • Acquire technically adequate images across all required core exams (probe selection, orientation, windows, optimization).
    • Distinguish normal sonographic anatomy from common emergency pathology.
    • Communicate findings clearly to patients and care teams and document accurately in Qpath.

    Student Responsibilities

    • 6–8 hours of Monday Image Review Q&A.
    • 1 hour of ultrasound-guided vascular access gel-model training.
    • Minimum 200 adequate, accurately interpreted POCUS exams.
    • 26 assigned Butterfly online modules.
    • 10 ultrasound quiz questions + 1 case or journal article presentation.
    • End-of-rotation computerized exam + faculty written evaluation.
